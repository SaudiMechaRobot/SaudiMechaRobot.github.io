## A guest from Hong Kong

In November 2025 the club welcomed **Eday Ver.Ed**, known among hobbyists as **Eddy**, to **Riyadh**. A **GBWC** champion, model designer and competition judge from **Hong Kong**, he is one of Asia's leading professionals and the author of the **Ver.Ed Anthology** book series on creative **Gunpla** customisation.

His workshop was the first technical workshop dedicated to **Gunpla** building in Saudi Arabia, as the club and **Eddy** announced together. His visit was also our first practical step toward our goal: to be a cultural bridge between **Saudi Arabia**, **Japan** and the world, and to bring professionals' expertise directly to hobbyists in the Kingdom.

### The workshop: the philosophy of modelling

The workshop was not about tools or techniques, since that information is available to everyone today. **Eddy** focused on questions that are rarely asked:

- What is creativity?
- How does creativity work?
- How do we turn a creative idea into a finished model?

Rather than lecturing, he started from the participants' own ideas and broke down with them the “formula” behind a custom build. He later explained the idea in a [detailed post](https://www.instagram.com/p/DSH-7LMj5_K/) that included feedback from the Saudi participants.

The workshop had three stages:

1. **Concepts:** from philosophy and building an idea to colour balance and precise detailing, including how to choose the right details and balance a piece.
2. **Demonstration:** **Eddy** applied design “formulas” to parts in front of us, from simple shapes to complex ideas ([clip](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DRnMr5kje07/)).
3. **Hands-on practice:** each participant picked a random part and built a specific shape from it using the concepts they had learned ([clip](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DRX2KjbjYnE/)).

Our main takeaway: **creativity starts with observation.**

## What the visit left with us

Members described the workshop as a real brainstorming session. Many said it changed how they see creativity and encouraged them to try new things in their builds.

For his part, **Eddy** wrote that the participants' enthusiasm and eagerness to learn exceeded his expectations. One thing that surprised him most was that there are women in Saudi Arabia who build **Gunpla**.

Most importantly, the visit proved that hosting professionals from outside the Kingdom is possible, and that our community is ready for it. We are still applying what we learned and returning to the **Ver.Ed Anthology** books.

## Thank you, Eddy

We thank **Eddy** for coming to Riyadh and for sharing his knowledge, passion and generous gifts with our members. We look forward to his next visit, and to hosting more professionals from **Japan** and around the world.
