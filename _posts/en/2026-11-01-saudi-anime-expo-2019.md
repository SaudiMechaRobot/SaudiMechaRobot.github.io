In November 2019, as part of **Riyadh Season**, Saudi Arabia hosted its first official government-organized anime event: **SAUDI ANIME EXPO 2019**. Held from November 14–16 at **Riyadh Front Expo Land Hall 3/4**, it attracted around **37,800 visitors** over three days and was widely regarded as the largest event of its kind in the Middle East at the time.

We always look back on this event, because it was **the strongest and most complete** of its kind, and it gave us special memories we lived **for the first time in Saudi Arabia**!

This was not just another commercial expo filled with the latest popular characters. It covered a wide range of unique angles, and much of it felt aimed at the older generation of fans — the ones who grew up with classic theme songs, giant robots and Showa-era anime. For many hardcore fans, it felt like the event was made for them.

## The Unforgettable Concert: Sasaki, Mizuki and Sami Clark

The highlight for many attendees was the appearance of Japanese anisong legends **Isao Sasaki** and **Ichiro Mizuki**, performing alongside Lebanese singer **Sami Clark**.

<figure class="post-figure"><img src="/assets/images/blog/anime-expo-2019-legends-grendizer.webp" alt="Ichiro Mizuki and Isao Sasaki in front of a Grendizer figure at the expo" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Ichiro Mizuki and Isao Sasaki in front of Grendizer at the expo</figcaption></figure>

On the final day, the three performed with Egypt's **Chromatic Orchestra**. They sang classic tracks including **Grendizer**, **Mazinger Z**, **Astroganger** and **Treasure Island (Takarajima)**, switching between Arabic and Japanese, and the crowd sang along throughout.

Mizuki greeted the audience in Arabic and told them, "I've waited for tens of years to come to Saudi Arabia." Sami Clark, wearing a Grendizer jacket, said, "The world would envy me because of you." Before the show ended, Mizuki told Clark, "If it weren't for Sammy singing all these songs, we wouldn't have been standing here with you."

Many fans from Japan, France and Italy attended the concert. Years later, many still talk about it and share the YouTube video of the performance. It was a rare moment that brought the original Japanese songs and the Arabic versions many in the region grew up with together on the same stage.

## Exhibits and Characters: Classic and Modern Side by Side

The venue featured a mix of classic and modern content. **Grendizer** and **Captain Tsubasa (Captain Majed)** were prominently present, *Gundam Narrative* was screened, and there were appearances or booths for **Space Battleship Yamato**, **Initial D**, **Samurai Champloo**, **Saint Seiya**, **Lupin the 3rd**, **Chibi Maruko-chan**, **Aizenborg** and **Code Geass**, alongside contemporary series such as One Piece, Attack on Titan, Naruto, Dragon Ball and Detective Conan.

**Sunrise** dedicated a booth to Gundam's 40th anniversary (**Gundam 40th Anniversary – Beyond**), with a giant **RX-78-2** head, an **RX-78-2 cockpit**, a wall tracing the history of the Gundam series, and 1/10 scale **Zaku II** and **Char's Zaku** figures.

The expo included roughly 70 anime characters, exhibits from 41 Japanese companies, cosplay areas, a movie theater zone and food areas. It also featured Saudi Arabia's first Ultraman live show, a talk with *Captain Tsubasa* creator **Yoichi Takahashi**, and performances by **Linked Horizon** and **FLOW**.

## The Iron Man (Aizenborg)

One of the most impressive things was seeing **Aizenborg** — known in Arabic as "The Iron Man" — in its real form, along with the **actual models** used to film the series. A special filmed presentation was screened **exclusively** at the event, and through it we learned about the documentary work by **Jarrah Al-Furaih** ([@aizen_fleed](https://www.instagram.com/aizen_fleed){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"}), producer of the film **"The Return of the Iron Man"** with Japan's **Tsuburaya Productions**.

## Why It Felt Made for Hardcore Fans

The event didn't only focus on the biggest current titles. There was clear recognition of the generation that carried anime in the region through Arabic theme songs and classic robot series. Bringing Sasaki and Mizuki together with Sami Clark was not accidental — it was a celebration of the shared memory between Japan and the Arab world.

The expo was part of **Vision 2030**'s push to diversify the economy and expand entertainment. It was the first official Japanimation event organized by the Saudi government in this form, through the **General Entertainment Authority** in partnership with **Dentsu**.

## Closing

Saudi Anime Expo 2019 was more than an entertainment event. It was a moment that connected the past and the present, Japan and Saudi Arabia, and the older generation of fans with the new one. For hardcore fans, it was a reminder that classic works are still valued, and that the voices of Grendizer, Mazinger and Yamato can still fill a hall in Riyadh decades later.

Do you remember that night? And do those songs still live with you today?
